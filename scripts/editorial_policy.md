# Fintech Pulse — editorial and pronunciation policy

Write a ten-minute Arabic conversation for a broad audience interested in Saudi
and global fintech. The show is NOT tailored to one banking product manager.
Do not repeatedly redirect news into merchant acquiring, POS, settlement, or
corporate onboarding. Cover those only when genuinely central to that day's story.

Research first in authoritative Arabic AND English sources. Preserve a fact sheet
with publication dates, event dates, URLs and exactly what each source supports.
Then compose in clear Saudi Arabic with light Najdi phrasing, not literal English
translation and not exaggerated dialect. Do not invent facts, citations, dates,
figures, quotations, or opinions attributed to real people. Separate fact from
the hosts' interpretation and use conditional wording for possible consequences.

## Voices and language

- سلطان: curious, warm, notices practical implications. Not perpetually sceptical.
- فيصل: thoughtful, clear, can challenge assumptions without lecturing.
- The ident is exactly "Fintech Pulse." in English, spoken by Faisal. Never spell
  the brand فنتك بلس. The ident is a separate asset; do not repeat it in the script.
- Use normal written Arabic vocabulary with light وش / طيب / بس / خلنا / ترى
  where natural. Neither host switches to Egyptian, Levantine or another dialect.
- All spoken numbers, percentages and dates use clear FORMAL Arabic words with
  correct agreement: "خمسة عشر تطبيقًا", "خمسة عشر في المئة", "ثلاثة ملايين ريال".
  Never خمستعش / خمسطعش / خمستعشر. Digits are fine in show notes, not spoken lines.
- Use selective diacritics only where pronunciation is ambiguous. Do not invent
  phonetic spellings for ordinary words. English product names and familiar
  technical terms are welcome, but explain unfamiliar terms once in plain Arabic.
- Foreign amounts need an approximate SAR equivalent. Verify the currency first
  (USD is not CAD). For USD use the verified dollar peg; for other currencies use
  a sourced, dated exchange rate. Record original amount, rate, date, source and
  calculation in the notes. If conversion cannot be verified, omit the amount,
  not the caveat. Never improvise a rate or mistake decimal units.

## Editorial spine and time budget

Choose one honest question that ties the episode together; do not force unrelated
stories into the same thesis. Cover two or three meaningful stories at most:
Saudi and global coverage, followed by one real product spotlight. When news is
quiet, say so briefly and spend the time teaching; never pad with stale headlines.

Aim for 9:30–10:30 INCLUDING existing music and ident, centred on ten minutes:
- About 25 seconds: a concrete hook, today's date, the question and what we'll learn.
- About 4:30: two or three news discussions. For each: what happened, why it matters,
  a practical example, and a genuine limit or unanswered question.
- About 4:15: Fintech Product Spotlight. One named real product not already covered.
  Explain who uses it, the problem, a customer journey, how it earns money, strengths,
  limitations, direct competitors (not a long list of vaguely related companies),
  and one reusable lesson. Verify product and pricing claims on official sources;
  if pricing is not public, say that instead of guessing a subscription model.
- About 30 seconds: close the opening question with one useful takeaway and what
  to watch next publishing day. Never say "tomorrow" on Thursday by default.

## A conversation, not performed spontaneity

Each reply must respond to the previous thought. Brief acknowledgments are fine
but avoid strings of "لا لا / لحظة / طيب طيب". Allow two or three sentences to finish
a thought. No mandatory argument, intentional factual error, forced change of mind,
invented personal experience, or interruption quota. A real distinction is more
engaging than fake conflict. Light humour is optional, never required.

Signal transitions with a meaningful bridge: finish one idea, explain the next
question, then move on. A clear "خلنا ناخذ مثال" is better than a random topic jump.
Avoid repeated summaries, long monologues, and chain reactions made of half-sentences.
No scripted overlap or truncation just to sound human. Normal conversational pauses
are healthy; never speed up or crossfade spoken words to hit duration.

Performance tags are optional and sparse: [curious], [thoughtful], [warm] only when
helpful. No acting directions for different accents, whispering, shouting or staged
laughter. The renderer supplies the consistent accent cue for each voice per block.

## Final editorial pass BEFORE audio

Read the complete script as a listener: can one follow the opening question through
news, example and takeaway? Cut tangents, not the ending. Check every claim against
the fact sheet; ensure older context is labelled and dates fall inside the requested
window. Check formal number grammar and SAR arithmetic. An automatic word check
cannot certify accent or factual accuracy. Keep the script, sources and audio blocks
so a bad segment can be repaired without regenerating the whole show.
