# Fintech Pulse Daily — Monday 24 August 2026

**Runtime:** ~12 minutes

## In this episode

**Cold open — tokenised equities go live.** Coinbase put tokenised shares of Apple, Nvidia, Meta and Alphabet live on Base using a purpose-built token standard, B20, with roughly 50 DeFi protocols supporting them on day one. B20 extends ERC-20 so existing wallets and routers work unchanged, and dividends and splits run through an on-chain multiplier so balances stay fixed and DeFi positions do not break. The tokens are offered under Regulation S and are not available to US persons; Robinhood's 2,000-plus stock tokens are EU/EEA only. The SEC's tokenisation "innovation exemption" remains delayed pending the Senate's 15 September CLARITY Act vote. Total RWA value on chain sat near $38.4bn on 21 August, with holders around 2.5 million — up roughly 93% over 30 days.

**Saudi.** Listed Saudi banks' net loans rose 7% to $865.2bn in Q2 — roughly three and a quarter trillion riyals. A separate earlier read put deposits near $835bn with bank credit up 7.3%; the hosts deliberately refuse to net the two figures against each other. Backdrop: SAMA has begun licensing fintech companies to provide open banking services, moving firms out of the sandbox and into supervised entity status.

**Global.** Treasury proposed the regulations implementing the GENIUS Act's central prohibition — who may issue, offer and sell payment stablecoins in the US. Issuance ban effective 18 January 2027; offer-and-sale cutoff for coins from unlicensed issuers in July 2028; comments close 19 October. Comptroller Gould has said the OCC's companion supervisory rule will be final by November. Same week: FASB proposed conditions under which a stablecoin counts as a cash equivalent on corporate balance sheets, and Standard Chartered became the first bank to distribute Hong Kong's HKDAP stablecoin.

**AI.** The Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance's 2026 Global AI in Financial Services Report draws on 628 respondents across 151 countries. On productivity impact by function, 203 fintechs and 149 traditional FIs were surveyed: fintechs report 86% positive impact in technology, data and product against 68% for traditional FIs. Risk and compliance was the only function where traditional institutions marginally edged ahead; both groups lag in corporate functions and leadership.

## Product spotlight — Marqeta

- **What it is:** an issuer processor with an API in front of it, sitting between a programme owner and the card networks.
- **What it does not remove:** the sponsor bank. Somebody still owns the BIN, the settlement account and the regulatory relationship.
- **Just-in-time funding:** at authorisation, Marqeta calls the programme owner's endpoint with merchant, amount and category; the owner approves and funds the card for that exact amount inside the authorisation window. Configure the fallback — approve or decline — before you sign.
- **Money:** mainly a share of interchange plus per-transaction and per-card processing fees. Thin take rate, volume business. Note the well-documented customer concentration in Block.
- **Where it breaks:** markets where interchange is regulated down, and buyers who mistake a processor for a full card programme (ledger, KYC, disputes, programme management all remain yours).
- **Competitors:** Galileo, Thredd, i2c, Enfuce, Pismo, Stripe Issuing, Adyen Issuing, Fiserv, FIS, NymCard, and the perennial in-house build.
- **Lesson:** the authorisation became a decision point rather than a balance lookup. The card is a trigger; the product is the rules engine behind it.
