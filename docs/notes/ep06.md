# Ep. 06 — Visa's Missing Partner and the Master Merchant Model
**Wednesday 26 August 2026 · Riyadh**

## Cold open — the networks buy the plumbing
Visa is looking for a new stablecoin settlement partner with licensing across the US, Canada, the UK and Singapore, per documents reported by CoinDesk. The role was previously BVNK's — Mastercard announced the up-to-$1.8bn acquisition in March and closed it on 3 August. The RFP covers coin swaps, settlement, OTC and support for **Open USD**, the multi-coin project fronted by Stripe, Visa and Mastercard.

## Product explained — Payment facilitation (the master merchant model)
**What it is.** A platform signs one merchant agreement with an acquirer, takes a single master merchant ID, and onboards businesses beneath it as *sub-merchants*. The sub-merchant contracts with the platform, not the bank.

**Problem solved.** Traditional merchant onboarding (CR, VAT certificate, ownership, risk review, MCC, terminal) takes days to weeks. PayFac collapses it to minutes inside the software the merchant already uses — and turns a fixed SaaS fee into a share of payment volume.

**Money flow.** Issuer authorises → scheme clears → acquirer settles **gross to the master merchant** → platform splits in its own sub-ledger (interchange, scheme fees, platform margin, reserves, refunds) → payout to each sub-merchant's IBAN.

**Risk.** Chargebacks land on the master MID; the platform is liable to the acquirer and recovers from the sub-merchant. Future-delivery exposure (deposits taken now, service later) is the core risk — hence reserves and rolling holdbacks. The bank's risk stops being diversified across thousands of merchants and concentrates in one counterparty.

**Providers.** Global: Stripe Connect, Adyen for Platforms, Square, Shopify, Toast, Lightspeed. Enablers: Finix, Infinicept, Payrix (Worldpay). Merchant-of-record: Paddle, FastSpring. Saudi/GCC: Foodics, Geidea, HyperPay, Moyasar, PayTabs, Telr, Tap; commerce platforms Salla and Zid.

**The SAMA line.** SAMA's *Rules for Dealing with E-Commerce Payment Service and Support Providers* treat linkage and technical support as supporting services that do not require a SAMA licence — technical permits come from Saudi Payments. Explicitly outside that: contracting directly with merchants; registering them and verifying data for KYC/AML/CFT and counter-fraud; finalising settlement; depositing funds into the merchant's bank account. The *Implementing Regulations of the Payments and Payment Services Law* define **Payment Aggregation Service** as facilitating payment on a pooled basis.

**Bank/PSP checklist.** Master merchant agreement with enforceable indemnity and callable reserve · API-based sub-merchant onboarding (register lookup, UBOs, sanctions screening) · sub-merchant-level risk monitoring · segregated settlement so merchant funds sit outside the platform's estate · dispute routing with evidence · portability/exit plan · right to inspect and right to suspend, in writing.
