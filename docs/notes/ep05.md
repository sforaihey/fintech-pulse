# Ep. 05 — 2026-08-27

# Banks Want Their Own Stablecoins, Saudi Factories Want Fintech Credit
**Fintech Pulse Daily — Episode 05, Thursday 27 August 2026 (Riyadh)**

## Saudi Arabia
- **Fintech lending to industry hits a record.** The Ministry of Industry and Mineral Resources reported that financing from Saudi fintech companies to industrial projects rose **130% in H1 2026 to SR541m (about $144m)**, up from SR234m in the same half of 2025. The half-year figure is already **about 70% of the whole of last year**. For context, 2025 full-year fintech-partnership lending was SR774m ($206.4m), itself up 36% on 2024.
- **Why a product manager should care:** this is receivables and purchase-order underwriting, not balance-sheet underwriting — the population is manufacturing SMEs that banks are slow to write facilities for.
- **Also this morning:** Saudi contractors secured **more than $2.6bn (just under SR10bn)** of projects in July; the Arab Monetary Fund reported Arab stock trading **volume up 29% in July while value traded fell**.

## Global
- **JPMorgan weighing a stablecoin** (WSJ, Wed 26 Aug) — preliminary, no product under development. It would sit alongside its existing tokenised deposit product. A **consortium of more than a dozen banks** (Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Santander among them) is advancing a **commercial stablecoin** in dollars, euros and other currencies.
- **BankChain Alliance** (announced Tue 25 Aug): an industry-owned blockchain network built around the Federal Home Loan Bank system — **~3,283 institutions, $21.8trn in assets (~SR82trn)** — supporting tokenised deposits, stablecoins and automated settlement, targeting **H1 2027**, interoperable with other chains.
- **Market reaction:** Circle and Coinbase shares slid Wednesday on the bank-competition read-through, with the **CLARITY Act** still in play — an earlier 2026 draft would have banned rewards on passive stablecoin balances, the trigger for Circle's worst day on record in March.
- **Separate effort:** Open USD, announced in June with 140+ members including Visa, Stripe, Mastercard, BlackRock, Coinbase and Google.
- **Europe:** the ECB published a speech on 26 August on moving from vision to delivery on a tokenised financial market.
- **Crypto context:** bitcoin slipped under $80,000 on Wednesday and traded around $79,000 Thursday morning.

## Product spotlight — Fireblocks
Digital-asset infrastructure for institutions: key management, transfer controls, a counterparty network and tooling for token issuance and stablecoin payouts.
- **Problem:** a private key is a bearer instrument; transfers are final; there is no chargeback and no Sunday-morning reversal.
- **Mechanism:** MPC — the key exists only as shares on separate devices held by different parties, signing jointly, never assembled in one place.
- **Flow:** workspace → vault accounts (per client / desk / purpose) → policy engine (asset, amount, destination, thresholds, named approvers) → co-signer approval on a separate device → whitelists and named counterparties → analytics screening on outbound → settlement, reporting and reconciliation.
- **Money:** subscription tiered by activity and volume, usage charges, add-on modules.
- **Limits:** it is technology, not a licence; single-provider uptime and chain-coverage concentration; a bad policy executes perfectly.
- **Competitors:** BitGo, Copper, Anchorage, Taurus, Zodia, Coinbase institutional, Ripple/Metaco, plus in-house HSM builds.
- **Lesson:** on irreversible rails, controls move to before the transaction. Measure time-to-add-a-counterparty and second-signature latency — slow approvals create workarounds.
