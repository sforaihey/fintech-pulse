## Product segment: Pay by Bank
**What it is.** A checkout method where the shopper pays by credit transfer from her own bank account, initiated by a licensed payment initiation provider. No card credential, no scheme network.

**Problem solved.** Cost (interchange + scheme fees + acquirer margin replaced by a near-zero marginal instant-transfer fee) and finality (push payment, irrevocable).

**The trade-off.** No chargeback scheme. Wins first where disputes are rare and average tickets are high: travel, wallet/brokerage funding, bill payment, B2B invoices.

**Money flow.** Shopper selects Pay by Bank → initiation provider constructs the instruction → shopper is handed into her own banking app and authenticates → her bank debits and pushes over the domestic instant rail (sarie in KSA; Faster Payments UK; SEPA Instant EU) → funds land in the merchant's account or a collecting PSP's account for payout. The initiation provider holds consent and an instruction, never the funds — which is why the licence class is lighter than e-money, and why margins are thin unless you also own settlement, reconciliation and refunds.

**Providers.** Global: Trustly, Volt, TrueLayer, Tink (Visa), GoCardless, Plaid (US). State-built A2A giants: Pix (Brazil), UPI (India). Regional: Lean Technologies, Tarabut (Riyadh), Fintech Galaxy (UAE).

**What a bank/PSP must do.** As payer's bank: compliant payment-initiation APIs, in-app authentication, and a refund path built in-house (no scheme to arbitrate). As acquirer/PSP: hold or partner for a payment-initiation permission, then rebuild reconciliation — matching inbound transfers to orders rather than settling a card batch. Merchant onboarding: same KYC file, but no chargeback liability means no rolling reserve on that volume.

**The disagreement on air.** Dana: mada is already cheap and card rewards hold shoppers. Adam: consumer pull never arrives on its own — merchant-funded discounts are what moved Pix; absent that, Pay by Bank stays a B2B and top-up product in the Kingdom.
